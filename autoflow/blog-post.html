Welcome to AutoFlow: Making Business Automation Simple

September 21, 20264 min readAnnouncement

Every business has a list of small tasks that need to happen again and again. A new order comes in, so someone copies the customer's details into a spreadsheet. A job is finished, so someone builds an invoice and emails it. An appointment is booked, so someone sends a reminder the day before. None of these tasks is difficult on its own, but together they take up time that could be spent serving customers, improving products, or simply running the business.

AutoFlow was created to take that work off your plate.

Why AutoFlow was created

Many small businesses already use good digital tools: an online form, an email account, a spreadsheet, an online store, or an invoicing app. The problem is that these tools often don't talk to each other. Information has to be moved from one place to another by hand, and every manual step is another chance for something to be delayed, forgotten, or typed incorrectly.

Larger companies often have technical teams to connect their systems. Smaller businesses usually don't. AutoFlow exists to close that gap by making automation affordable, understandable, and easy to buy — even if you have never written a line of code.

What business automation actually means

Business automation simply means setting up your tools so that routine tasks happen on their own when something specific occurs. Every automation has two parts:

  • A trigger — the event that starts it, such as a new order, a submitted form, or a set time each week.
  • Actions — the tasks that follow automatically, such as saving the customer, creating an invoice, sending an email, or updating a spreadsheet.

Here's an example. A customer places an order (the trigger). Their details are saved, an invoice is generated, a confirmation email is sent, and your sales sheet is updated (the actions). Nobody presses a button. The workflow handles it in the background, the same way every time.

Who AutoFlow is for

AutoFlow is designed for small businesses, entrepreneurs, online stores, freelancers, service businesses, and startups — anyone who spends too much time on repetitive digital tasks and would rather spend it elsewhere. You don't need technical knowledge. If you can describe how a task is done today, it can usually be turned into an automated workflow.

The services we offer

Our work is packaged into clear services with upfront, one-time setup prices, so you know exactly what you're getting:

  • Email Automation Setup ($99) — confirmations, reminders, and welcome messages sent automatically.
  • Spreadsheet Automation ($149) — form responses and business data added, organized, and calculated without copying and pasting.
  • Invoice Automation ($199) — invoices created and emailed whenever an order or service is completed.
  • Customer Follow-Up System ($249) — timed thank-you messages, review requests, and check-ins after every purchase or appointment.
  • Custom Business Workflow (from $499) — a tailored system that connects several tools and automates an entire process.

Each service includes setup and testing, so your workflow is checked before it goes live.

How automation reduces repetitive work

The value of automation isn't the technology itself — it's what you get back. When a workflow handles data entry, information is entered the same way every time, so there are fewer typos to fix later. When follow-ups are scheduled automatically, no customer is forgotten during a busy week. And when your records update themselves, you can trust that your spreadsheet reflects what actually happened. A few minutes saved on one task may not sound like much, but tasks that repeat many times a week add up quickly.
