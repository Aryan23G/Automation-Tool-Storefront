What Is Workflow Automation?

September 26, 20262 min readBasics

"Workflow automation" can sound technical, but the idea is simple. It means letting your software handle a sequence of routine steps for you, automatically, whenever a certain event happens.

Triggers and actions

Every workflow is built from two parts. A trigger is the event that starts it, such as a new order, a submitted form, a new customer, or a scheduled time. Actions are the steps that follow, such as saving information, sending an email, creating a document, or notifying a team member.

Some workflows also include delays (wait three days, then send a message) or simple conditions (only send a reminder if the invoice hasn't been paid).

A simple example

Imagine a customer fills out a booking form on your website. A workflow could save their details to your customer list, add the booking to a spreadsheet, and send them a confirmation email within moments, without anyone touching a keyboard.

What automation isn't

Workflow automation doesn't replace the people in your business. It replaces the repetitive parts of their day, so they can spend more time on work that needs a human touch: talking to customers, solving problems, and making decisions.

Getting started

Most businesses begin with a single workflow that removes one frustrating, repetitive task. Once that's running smoothly, it becomes much easier to see what else could be automated. Browse our automation services to see common starting points.
